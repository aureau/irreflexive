Fox Fires Maria Bartiromo After Text to Trump

(White House)

By Newsmax Wires    |   Friday, 04 September 2026 07:24 PM EDT

Add Newsmax On Google
Print|
    A   A

Fox News' decision to abruptly fire Maria Bartiromo, one of the network's most prominent and popular pro-Trump anchors, is stunning conservatives and reviving memories of the network's previous breakups with some of its biggest stars, including Tucker Carlson, Bill O'Reilly and Lou Dobbs.

Fox announced Thursday that Bartiromo was leaving after more than a decade with the network but offered no explanation for the decision.

"We thank Maria for her work over the last 12 ½ years and wish her all the best on her next chapter," Fox said.

The network separately characterized her departure as a "business decision." Bartiromo had not appeared on air since Aug. 9.

Multiple media reports say the immediate dispute involved Bartiromo allegedly sharing confidential Fox Business editorial guidance with senior White House officials.

According to reports cited by Variety and Mediaite, Fox Business executives instructed producers following President Donald Trump's July 16 prime-time address to not report on some of his key allegations involving China and the 2020 election.

Bartiromo reportedly took a screenshot of the internal guidance and sent it to White House officials.

Fox executives reportedly discovered what had happened after receiving a call from the White House.

Variety reported that divulging confidential company information to an outside party would likely constitute a violation of employment terms. Fox also parted ways with Bartiromo's executive producer, Patrick Ignozzi.

Trump's July address focused heavily on election security and China.

He alleged that China had obtained data involving some 220 million U.S. voters and accused Beijing of trying to undermine him in 2020.

Trump said China had created an "unprecedented election security nightmare," although some election experts disputed that claim.

The reported Fox directive came against the backdrop of the network's costly litigation over its coverage of the 2020 election.

Fox agreed in 2023 to pay Dominion Voting Systems $787.5 million to settle its defamation lawsuit. Bartiromo was among the Fox personalities whose broadcasts figured prominently in the litigation.

Fox, which had opposed Trump during the 2016 election and was less than supportive in his 2020 bid, sought to close down his aspirations to return to office after he left the White House in 2021.

After Trump lost his 2020 reelection bid, Rupert Murdoch, then chairman of Fox Corp., emailed a top executive: "We want to make Trump a non-person."

Asked in a 2023 deposition whether he stood by the statement, Murdoch responded, "I'd still like to." He also described Trump as "mad, maybe clinically."

For some of Fox's pro-Trump viewers, Bartiromo's sudden removal also recalls a succession of departures by highly rated personalities.

Fox abruptly parted ways with Tucker Carlson in April 2023, less than a week after the Dominion settlement.

The network never publicly provided a detailed explanation for firing its highest-rated prime-time host; subsequent reporting pointed to several factors, including internal communications and litigation involving a former producer.

Liberal media journalist Yashar Ali posted Thursday on X he was not surprised by Bartiromo's departure.

Ali wrote: "Maria Bartiromo has learned a lesson that others at Fox News have learned over the years: the patience of the Murdochs is not unlimited, and while they may be conservative and align with Trump in many areas, their ultimate loyalty is only to themselves and their own interests. It's a lesson Kimberly Guilfoyle, Lou Dobbs, Bill O'Reilly, Tucker Carlson, Roger Ailes, Bill Shine and others have learned the hard way."

Fox canceled Lou Dobbs' top-rated Fox Business program in February 2021, shortly after Smartmatic filed a $2.7 billion defamation lawsuit naming Fox, Dobbs, Bartiromo, and Jeanine Pirro.

Bill O'Reilly, once a dominant figure in cable news ratings, was forced out in 2017 following sexual harassment allegations and disclosures of settlements. O'Reilly denied the allegations.

The circumstances of those departures differed substantially, but together they have contributed to a perception among some conservatives that even Fox's biggest stars can be forced out after clashing with the network's leadership.

Bartiromo's long tenure at the network makes her departure notable.

She became a television business-news star during two decades at CNBC, gaining fame reporting from the floor of the New York Stock Exchange before moving to Fox in 2014.

She eventually became one of Fox's most prominent interviewers of Trump and senior administration officials.

Trump quickly rallied to her defense Thursday, calling Bartiromo a "true warrior" and a "total professional."

Fox, meanwhile, is moving quickly to rebrand Bartiromo's programs.

"Mornings With Maria" is becoming "Mornings With Fox Business," while "Maria Bartiromo's Wall Street" is being renamed "FBN's Wall Street."

Former Utah Rep. Jason Chaffetz will temporarily host "Sunday Morning Futures."
